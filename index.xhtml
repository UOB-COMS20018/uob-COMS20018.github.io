Unit Information

This unit introduces students to the foundations and contemporary landscape of Artificial Intelligence. We cover core machine learning concepts, large language models, causal inference, reinforcement learning and computer vision, alongside the philosophical foundations and ethical considerations that underpin the design of trustworthy AI systems.

Assessment Details

Students will be assessed via three elements:

  • A mid-term video presentation (worth 20% of the unit)
  • A series of laboratory-based assessments (worth 20% of the unit)
  • An end-of-term exam (worth 60% of the unit)

Lab Work

The labs are formative exercises which we strongly encourage you to complete, as they are designed to support your understanding of the material covered in lectures.

Using the machines in MVB 1.15

If you are using UoB machines to do the lab exercises (as opposed to using your own machine) you need to do the following.

  1. Make sure the machine you are using is running Linux (reboot if necessary).
  2. Open up a Terminal window so you have access to the Linux command line.
  3. Enter the following at the command line: module load anaconda/3-2025

Using your own machine

If you want to do the lab exercises on your own machine then you should install Anaconda on it. If you run into installation problems then feel free to ask the Teaching Staff on the unit for help, but we can't guarantee to solve them.

Reading Materials

Readings are listed below for weeks with finalised lecture content; materials for the remaining weeks will be added as those lectures are finalised.

Week 2: Machine Learning

  • Bishop, C. M., Pattern Recognition and Machine Learning (2006) – our main textbook for Weeks 2–3. Free online. Chapter 1 (§1.1), Chapter 3 (§3.1), Chapter 4 (§4.3.2), Chapter 5: (§5.1, §5.2, §5.3). Free online.

Week 3: Deep Learning

  • Bishop & Bishop, Deep Learning: Foundations and Concepts (2024)
  • Goodfellow, Bengio, Courville, Deep Learning (2016), Chapters 6 and 8 – a more modern treatment (ReLU, Adam, and optimisation for deep nets). Free online (optional, further reading).
  • Vaswani et al. “Attention Is All You Need.” NeurIPS, 2017 (the original Transformer paper).

Week 8: Causal Inference

  • Hernán & Robins, Causal Inference: What If (2020), Part I (Chapters 1–7). Free online.
  • Spirtes, Glymour, Scheines, Causation, Prediction, and Search (2000), Chapter 5 (constraint-based discovery), or Peters, Janzing, Schölkopf, Elements of Causal Inference (2017), Chapter 7 (overview). Free online (Peters et al.).

Week 9: Reinforcement Learning

  • Sutton & Barto, Reinforcement Learning: An Introduction (2nd ed.), Chapter 2: Multi-armed Bandits. Free online.
  • Lattimore & Szepesvári, Bandit Algorithms (2020), Chapters 1, 2 and 19 (linear bandits). Free online.
  • Li, Chu, Langford, Schapire. “A Contextual-Bandit Approach to Personalized News Article Recommendation.” WWW 2010 (for LinUCB).
  • Sutton & Barto, Reinforcement Learning: An Introduction (2nd ed.), Chapters 3 (MDPs), 6 (TD learning & Q-learning) and 13 (Policy Gradient methods). Free online.
